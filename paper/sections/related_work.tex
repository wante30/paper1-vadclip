\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Weakly Supervised Video Anomaly Detection}
The multiple-instance formulation of Sultani et al.~\cite{sultani2018real} supports temporal anomaly scoring from video-level supervision. Subsequent work improves the evidence used for scoring: graph-based label cleaning addresses noisy snippet labels~\cite{zhong2019gcnlnc}, RTFM learns discriminative feature magnitudes~\cite{tian2021rtfm}, MGFN combines magnitude contrast with context modeling~\cite{chen2022mgfn}, and UR-DMU regulates uncertainty through dual memories~\cite{zhou2023urdmu}. HL-Net models similarity and temporal-distance relations for the XD-Violence benchmark~\cite{wu2020xdviolence}. Our method works with frozen snippet features and targets the interface between variable-length sequences and fixed-length temporal learning.

\subsection{Vision-Language Models for Anomaly Detection}
CLIP-TSA applies temporal self-attention to CLIP features~\cite{joo2023cliptsa}, while PEL4VAD integrates prompt-enhanced context learning~\cite{pu2023pel4vad}. VadCLIP adds prompt-aligned semantic prediction to the classification pathway~\cite{wu2024vadclip}. Normality-guided prompts~\cite{yang2024tpwng} and spatio-temporal prompts~\cite{wu2024stprompt} further enrich anomaly semantics. VadCLIP++ learns dynamic text prompts using adjacent-frame difference information and combines them with spatial feature selection~\cite{liu2026vadclippp}. Its use of temporal differences concerns dynamic prompt learning. Our temporal refinement instead computes variation before feature aggregation, and our semantic refinement controls which existing parameters an auxiliary objective may update. Language-guided methods also support training-free anomaly scoring~\cite{zanella2024lavad} and multimodal anomaly reasoning~\cite{xu2025customizing}. We focus on a lightweight, trainable extension of a snippet-scoring detector.

\subsection{Temporal Cues and Lightweight Adaptation}
Explicit temporal cues are well established in video understanding. TDN uses frame differences~\cite{wang2021tdn}, TSM exchanges temporal information through channel shifts~\cite{lin2019tsm}, and X-CLIP introduces cross-frame communication~\cite{ni2022xclip}. Here, the input is already encoded by a frozen backbone, making the ordering of feature differencing and temporal aggregation the relevant design choice. Graph convolution~\cite{kipf2017gcn} provides the relation-propagation mechanism refined by our kNN mixture.

Adapters~\cite{houlsby2019adapter}, low-rank updates~\cite{hu2021lora}, prompt learning~\cite{zhou2022coop}, CLIP feature adapters~\cite{gao2024clipadapter}, and ST-Adapter~\cite{pan2022stadapter} demonstrate efficient adaptation of pretrained representations. Our two stages use this efficiency in different ways: a small residual introduces missing temporal statistics, whereas semantic adaptation reuses an existing adapter. The latter is selected by its downstream dependency, which makes classification preservation a structural property rather than a consequence of balancing loss weights.
