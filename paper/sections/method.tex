\section{Method}
\subsection{Dual-Output Detection Pipeline}
Let $X\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d}$ denote a video's raw CLIP snippet features, where $L$ is variable and $d=512$. During training, $\Agg$ maps each sequence to $T=256$ positions: sequences longer than $T$ are averaged over uniform temporal segments, and shorter sequences are zero-padded. VadCLIP's local temporal transformer, similarity graph branch, and distance graph branch transform the input into a shared representation $h\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times d}$~\cite{wu2024vadclip}. The classification logits are
\begin{equation}
 s^{(1)}=f_{\mathrm{cls}}\big(h+\mlp_2(h)\big).
 \label{eq:classifier}
\end{equation}
The semantic pathway uses these logits to form a normalized video vector and condition each class prompt:
\begin{align}
 a &= \Norm\!\left(\sum_j s^{(1)}_j h_j\right), \label{eq:pool}\\
 u_c &= t_c^0+a, \qquad t_c=u_c+\mlp_1(u_c), \label{eq:prompt}\\
 s^{(2)}_{j,c}&=\frac{\cos(h_j,t_c)}{\tau},\qquad
 p_j=1-\softmax\!\left(s^{(2)}_j\right)_{\mathrm{normal}},
 \label{eq:semantic}
\end{align}
where $\Norm$ is $\ell_2$ normalization, $t_c^0$ is the encoded class prompt, and $\tau=0.07$. The classification output in Eq.~\eqref{eq:classifier} is independent of $\mlp_1$, while the semantic output depends on classification through $a$. This directed dependency is central to Stage~2. Following VadCLIP, the headline metrics use $s^{(1)}$ on UCF-Crime and $p$ on XD-Violence.

\subsection{Pre-Aggregation Variation Refinement}
\textbf{Aligned temporal evidence.}
For a long sequence, let $\mathcal{B}_j$ be the raw snippet indices assigned to aggregated position $j$. The appearance input is the segment mean $A_j=|\mathcal{B}_j|^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}_j}X_t$. To retain explicit variation statistics, we first compute
\begin{equation}
 D_1=0,\qquad D_t=|X_t-X_{t-1}|,\quad t=2,\ldots,L,
 \label{eq:variation}
\end{equation}
with elementwise absolute value. We then aggregate both streams using the same boundaries:
\begin{equation}
 A=\Agg(X),\qquad V=\Agg(D).
 \label{eq:aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus $V_j$ summarizes raw feature changes assigned to segment $j$, including a boundary transition when its first snippet follows another segment. Concatenating the appearance and variation channels before temporal processing keeps their positions aligned.

This ordering extracts information that differences of segment means do not specify. For example, a constant feature trajectory and an alternating trajectory around the same center can have identical means but different average absolute changes. After aggregation, a temporal difference sees the means alone. Computing Eq.~\eqref{eq:variation} first exposes the variation statistic to the detector alongside appearance. In our long training sequences, aggregation averages 5.76 snippets per segment on UCF-Crime and 2.23 on XD-Violence. On shorter sequences, pre- and post-aggregation differences coincide at valid positions before padding.

\textbf{Residual integration.}
The aligned variation stream is mapped back to the appearance space with a low-rank residual:
\begin{equation}
 R(V)=W_2\,\mathrm{GELU}\!\left(W_1\,\mathrm{LN}(V)\right),
 \qquad Z=A+\beta R(V),
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
where the projection widths are $512\rightarrow128\rightarrow512$, biases are implicit, and $\beta=0.2$. The second projection and its bias are zero-initialized, so the input residual initially vanishes: $Z=A$. This initialization lets the model learn a variation correction from the original appearance input. $Z$ is injected before positional embeddings and the temporal transformer, allowing both prediction pathways to use the refined representation. The residual adds 132,736 parameters.

At inference, raw appearance and variation features are split into the same non-overlapping 256-snippet windows, with padding in the final window. No segment averaging is performed. Variation is computed over the full raw sequence before windowing, preserving transitions across adjacent windows in the variation input. Training and inference therefore share the feature-space difference and residual mapping while retaining VadCLIP's windowed evaluation.

\subsection{Sparse-Neighborhood Graph Refinement}
We strengthen similarity-based propagation with a parameter-free nearest-neighbor mixture. Let $F$ be the transformer output and $S_{ij}=\cos(F_i,F_j)$. VadCLIP thresholds similarities at $0.7$ before applying a row-wise softmax~\cite{wu2024vadclip}. A thresholded zero still contributes $e^0$ to the softmax denominator, so these entries retain nonzero weights. A sparse neighborhood term concentrates part of the propagation mass on the most similar snippets:
\begin{equation}
 G_{\mathrm{kNN}}=\softmax\!\big(M_k(S)\big),\qquad
 G=(1-\alpha)G_{\mathrm{orig}}+\alpha G_{\mathrm{kNN}},
 \label{eq:graph}
\end{equation}
where $M_k$ retains the $k$ largest similarities per valid row, including self, and masks the others to $-\infty$. We use $k=16$ and $\alpha=0.25$. The mixture retains global context through $G_{\mathrm{orig}}$ and increases the contribution of the nearest-neighbor set through $G_{\mathrm{kNN}}$. Both terms are row-normalized on valid positions. The final mixture retains a dense component; the sparse term changes the allocation of propagation weights without introducing trainable parameters. Only the similarity branch uses $G$. The distance branch and the concatenation/projection that produce $h$ remain as in VadCLIP.

\subsection{Pathway-Restricted Semantic Adaptation}
\textbf{Semantic multiple-instance objective.}
VadCLIP supervises prompt-aligned logits with its class-level CLASM objective~\cite{wu2024vadclip}. We supplement it with a binary anomaly objective applied to the semantic probability $p$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:semantic}. For valid sequence length $\ell$, define $k_b=\lfloor\ell/16\rfloor+1$ and let $\mathcal{K}$ contain the $k_b$ highest-scoring valid positions. With video-level anomaly label $y$, the objective is
\begin{equation}
 q=\frac{1}{k_b}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}p_j,\qquad
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{S2}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CLASM}}+
 \lambda\,\BCE(q,y),
 \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda=0.1$. CLASM retains class-level alignment, while the binary term directly supervises the anomalousness represented by $p$. Both use the original video-level labels.

\textbf{Classification-preserving update.}
Stage~2 starts from the Stage~1 checkpoint and optimizes Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss} with respect to $\theta_{\mlp_1}$ alone. All other parameters are frozen, including the temporal network, classification head, CLIP encoders, and prompt embeddings. The frozen components run in evaluation mode. Since $\mlp_1$ is downstream of the classifier,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial s^{(1)}}{\partial\theta_{\mlp_1}}=0.
 \label{eq:invariance}
\end{equation}
For a fixed input, $h$, $s^{(1)}$, $a$, and $t_c^0$ are therefore unchanged during this stage. Semantic predictions can still adapt through $t_c=u_c+\mlp_1(u_c)$. This restriction separates semantic optimization from classification preservation without requiring a compensating classification loss or a choice of trade-off weight between the two outputs. It preserves the classifier established by Stage~1, including any downstream thresholding of its scores. Stage~2 updates 2,099,712 existing parameters and adds no inference operations or parameters.
