\section{Preliminaries: VadCLIP}
\label{sec:prelim}

VadCLIP~\cite{wu2024vadclip} detects anomalies from frozen CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} snippet features $X \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}$, where $L$ is the number of snippets and $d=512$. During training, each video is mapped to a fixed length $T=256$ by an operator $\mathrm{Agg}(\cdot)$: videos with $L > T$ are divided into $T$ uniform segments and each segment is mean-pooled, and shorter videos are zero-padded. At test time the sequence is cut into non-overlapping windows of length $T$ without pooling.

The aggregated features pass through a local temporal transformer and two graph convolution branches, one built on feature similarity and one on temporal distance~\cite{wu2020xdviolence}. Their output $h \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d}$ is the shared representation from which both predictions are computed.

The first output is a snippet-level anomaly logit,
\begin{equation}
  s^{(1)} = f_{\mathrm{cls}}\big(h + \mathrm{mlp}_2(h)\big),
  \label{eq:logits1}
\end{equation}
trained with a top-$k$ multiple-instance objective~\cite{sultani2018real}. The second output aligns $h$ with class prompts. VadCLIP pools $h$ with weights given by $s^{(1)}$ into a video vector $a$, adds it to each prompt embedding $t^0_c$ from the CLIP text encoder, and refines the result with a text-side adapter:
\begin{equation}
  t_c = (t^0_c + a) + \mathrm{mlp}_1(t^0_c + a), \qquad
  s^{(2)}_{j,c} = \frac{\cos(h_j, t_c)}{\tau},
  \label{eq:logits2}
\end{equation}
with $\tau = 0.07$. The semantic anomaly probability of snippet $j$ is $p_j = 1 - \mathrm{softmax}(s^{(2)}_{j})_{\mathrm{normal}}$, and $s^{(2)}$ is trained with the CLASM objective $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CLASM}}$~\cite{wu2024vadclip}.

The two outputs form a cascade: $s^{(2)}$ depends on $s^{(1)}$ through $a$, and $s^{(1)}$ is independent of $\mathrm{mlp}_1$. Following VadCLIP, UCF-Crime is reported with AUC computed from $s^{(1)}$ and XD-Violence with AP computed from $p$.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Our method trains VadCLIP in two stages. Both datasets use the same method design and the same module hyperparameters, and each keeps VadCLIP's dataset-specific training configuration. Stage~1 changes the shared representation $h$: it adds temporal variation computed before aggregation (Sec.~\ref{sec:pvr}) and a sparse refinement of the similarity graph (Sec.~\ref{sec:graph}). Stage~2 starts from the Stage~1 model and trains a semantic multiple-instance objective on $\mathrm{mlp}_1$ alone, which keeps the additional optimization in the semantic pathway (Sec.~\ref{sec:isa}). Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes both stages.

\subsection{Pre-aggregation variation residual}
\label{sec:pvr}

For a long training video, $\mathrm{Agg}$ averages consecutive snippets within each segment, which averages away the changes between snippets inside a segment. Differencing the pooled sequence measures change between segment means. In our training sets, \ucfTrainAvgFrac\% of UCF-Crime and \xdTrainAvgFrac\% of XD-Violence items are longer than $T$, with on average \ucfBinSize{} and \xdBinSize{} snippets per segment respectively.

We therefore compute variation on the raw snippet sequence, before pooling:
\begin{equation}
  D_1 = 0, \qquad D_t = \lvert X_t - X_{t-1} \rvert, \quad t = 2, \dots, L,
  \label{eq:diff}
\end{equation}
and aggregate it with the same segment boundaries as the appearance stream, $A = \mathrm{Agg}(X)$ and $V = \mathrm{Agg}(D)$. At test time both streams are windowed in the same way. The variation statistics enter through a low-rank residual,
\begin{equation}
  R(V) = W_2\, \mathrm{GELU}\big(W_1\, \mathrm{LN}(V)\big), \qquad
  Z = A + \beta\, R(V),
  \label{eq:pvr}
\end{equation}
with $W_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{128 \times d}$ and $W_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times 128}$. $W_2$ and its bias are initialized to zero, so training starts from the unmodified VadCLIP input, and $\beta = 0.2$. $Z$ replaces $A$ as input to the temporal transformer, so the residual reaches both outputs. The module adds \pvrParams{} parameters.

\subsection{Sparse neighbourhood refinement of the similarity graph}
\label{sec:graph}

VadCLIP builds the similarity graph $G_{\mathrm{orig}}$ from cosine similarities, sets entries below $0.7$ to zero, and applies a row-wise softmax. Zeroed entries still receive weight $e^0$ after the softmax, so every snippet keeps a non-negligible connection to all others. Within Stage~1 we mix this graph with a sparse $k$-nearest-neighbour graph:
\begin{equation}
  G = (1-\alpha)\, G_{\mathrm{orig}} + \alpha\, \mathrm{softmax}\big(M_k(S)\big),
  \label{eq:graph}
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the cosine similarity matrix and $M_k$ keeps the $k$ largest entries of each row (including the snippet itself) and sets the rest to $-\infty$. We use $k = 16$ and $\alpha = 0.25$. $G$ replaces $G_{\mathrm{orig}}$ in the similarity branch. The distance branch and the parameter count are unchanged. Section~\ref{sec:ablation} examines this refinement with and without the variation residual.

\subsection{Pathway-restricted semantic post-training}
\label{sec:isa}

CLASM supervises $s^{(2)}$ through class-level top-$k$ pooling. A binary multiple-instance loss on $p$ adds a direct video-level anomaly target. For a video with valid length $\ell$, let $q$ be the mean of the $k' = \lfloor \ell/16 \rfloor + 1$ largest values of $p_j$ and $y \in \{0,1\}$ its video label:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{G} = \mathrm{BCE}(q, y), \qquad
  \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CLASM}} + \lambda\, \mathcal{L}_{G},
  \label{eq:isa}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda = 0.1$. When we trained the whole network with this loss added, the gradient also changed $h$ and therefore $s^{(1)}$. In a single-seed run, this raised XD-Violence AP by \fullGXdApTwo{} points and changed UCF-Crime AUC by \fullGUcfAucOne{} points relative to the Stage~1 model.

Stage~2 optimizes Eq.~\eqref{eq:isa} with respect to $\theta_{\mathrm{mlp}_1}$ only. Because $s^{(1)}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:logits1} is independent of $\mathrm{mlp}_1$, the classification output is unchanged by construction, while $p$ can still adapt. All other parameters are frozen and the rest of the network runs in evaluation mode. Stage~2 updates \mlpOneParams{} existing parameters for three epochs with AdamW (learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$, weight decay $0.01$, no scheduler) and adds no parameters or inference cost.

\subsection{Implementation details}
\label{sec:impl}

Stage~1 follows the released VadCLIP training configuration for each dataset, including its loss weights, for 10 epochs, and we evaluate the final-epoch checkpoint. Stage~2 uses batch size 64 on UCF-Crime and 96 on XD-Violence. The hyperparameters $\beta$, $k$, $\alpha$, and $\lambda$ are shared across datasets. Section~\ref{sec:complexity} reports inference cost.
