\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Video anomaly detection supports intelligent surveillance and safety monitoring, where frame-level annotation of rare events is too costly to collect at scale. Weakly supervised video anomaly detection (WSVAD) therefore learns to localize abnormal events from video-level labels alone, typically by training a light temporal model on frozen snippet features with a multiple-instance objective~\cite{sultani2018real}. CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} features have become the standard input, and VadCLIP~\cite{wu2024vadclip} extends this pipeline with a second, prompt-aligned semantic output. It is a common base for recent work~\cite{yang2024tpwng,wu2024stprompt}.

We identify two problems of placement in this pipeline. First, the temporal cue is extracted too late. Each training video is mapped to a fixed length before learning, and long videos are mean-pooled over uniform segments, which affects \ucfTrainAvgFrac\% of UCF-Crime and \xdTrainAvgFrac\% of XD-Violence training videos. Pooling averages away the change between snippets inside a segment, and any difference taken afterwards measures change between segment means. Second, semantic optimization is applied too broadly. VadCLIP's semantic output depends on its classification output, and both read the same representation, so a semantic objective trained over the whole network also moves the classification output.

The key question is therefore where refinement is applied: temporal cues should be preserved before aggregation, while semantic adaptation should be restricted after the prediction pathways separate. We follow this principle with two designs. Pre-aggregation temporal refinement computes snippet differences on the raw sequence, aggregates them with the same boundaries as the appearance stream, and adds them through a zero-initialized low-rank residual, with a sparse neighbourhood refinement of the similarity graph as support. Pathway-restricted semantic adaptation optimizes a semantic multiple-instance objective only on VadCLIP's text-side adapter, which lies after the pathway split, so the classification output is unchanged.

Compared with VadCLIP retrained under the same protocol and matched seeds, our method improves UCF-Crime AUC by \ucfGainMean{} points and XD-Violence AP by \xdGainMean{} points, positive on every seed. The improvement persists under VadCLIP's official training setting, and the method adds \pvrParams{} parameters and about 3\% latency, which suits lightweight deployment. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Pre-aggregation temporal variation.} We show that snippet variation extracted before fixed-length aggregation improves a CLIP-based detector, whereas the same variation extracted after aggregation degrades it.
  \item \emph{Pathway-restricted semantic adaptation.} We restrict additional semantic optimization to the semantic-side adapter, which improves the semantic output while preserving the classification pathway exactly and adding no parameters.
  \item \emph{Controlled validation.} We evaluate both designs with matched multi-seed comparisons and mechanism controls, and confirm robustness under VadCLIP's official training setting.
\end{itemize}
