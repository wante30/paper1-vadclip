\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\textbf{Datasets and metrics.}
We evaluate on UCF-Crime~\cite{sultani2018real} and XD-Violence~\cite{wu2020xdviolence} using VadCLIP's released CLIP features, dataset splits, and frame-level evaluation~\cite{wu2024vadclip}. Their test sets contain 290 and 800 videos, respectively. The headline metrics are frame-level AUC from the classification output on UCF-Crime and frame-level AP from the semantic output on XD-Violence. We also evaluate both outputs on UCF-Crime and classification AP on XD-Violence. Scores are percentages; improvements are percentage points.

\textbf{Training.}
Stage~1 retains the original dataset-specific configuration and losses for 10 epochs. UCF-Crime uses two temporal transformer layers, attention window 8, learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$, and batch size 64. XD-Violence uses one layer, window 64, learning rate $10^{-5}$, and batch size 96. Stage~2 uses three epochs of AdamW with learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$, weight decay $0.01$, and the same dataset-specific batch sizes. The method hyperparameters $\beta=0.2$, $k=16$, $\alpha=0.25$, and $\lambda=0.1$ are shared across datasets.

\textbf{Evaluation settings.}
For controlled comparisons and ablations, we evaluate the final checkpoint after the fixed training budget; test data are not used in training or checkpoint selection. Runs use seeds 234--236, with the same seed and sample order for each comparison. The component study with PVR alone uses the available matched seeds 234/235. Separately, official-setting experiments on both datasets retain VadCLIP's original training and test-metric-based checkpoint selection and reload procedures. Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} reports the best of three runs on each dataset, and Table~\ref{tab:official} summarizes the matched comparisons.

\subsection{Detection Performance}
\textbf{Published benchmark comparison.}
Across the three official-setting runs in Table~\ref{tab:official}, UCF-Crime mean AUC increases from 87.63\% to 87.98\%, with a paired gain of $0.35\pm0.03$ points. On XD-Violence, mean AP increases from 82.91\% to 83.63\%, with a paired gain of $0.73\pm0.16$ points. Gains are positive in all three seeds on each dataset. Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} places the best-run results of 88.25\% AUC and 84.94\% AP among representative CLIP-feature results. The corresponding same-seed baselines are 87.90\% AUC and 84.21\% AP, respectively. The paired comparisons establish consistent improvements under the original training settings.

\textbf{Controlled comparisons.}
Table~\ref{tab:controlled} evaluates the complete two-stage model against retrained VadCLIP with the fixed-epoch setting. UCF-Crime AUC increases from 86.25\% to 86.66\%, and XD-Violence AP from 77.57\% to 79.23\%. The respective paired improvements are $0.41\pm0.11$ and $1.66\pm0.34$ points, positive in all three runs. The table also exposes the effect beyond the headline UCF metric: classification AP improves by 0.78 points, semantic AUC by 0.44, and semantic AP by 0.85. Thus, the UCF improvement is reflected in the mean ranking metrics of both outputs.

On XD-Violence, classification AP is 79.54\% for the complete method and 79.61\% for the baseline, while semantic AP provides the principal improvement. This distinction matches the design of the framework: Stage~1 changes the representation read by both outputs, and Stage~2 targets semantic scores while preserving the Stage~1 classifier. The following ablations identify the contribution of each operation.

\subsection{Component and Mechanism Ablations}
\textbf{PVR independently improves the detector.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) compares VadCLIP, PVR alone, and PVR with the kNN mixture using the same two seeds. PVR raises UCF-Crime AUC from 86.44\% to 86.69\%, a gain of 0.25 points without graph refinement. Adding the kNN term further increases AUC to 86.80\%. Its incremental gains over PVR are 0.16 and 0.06 points for the two seeds. This component study establishes the standalone contribution of temporal variation and the additional benefit of refining its similarity-based propagation.

\textbf{Pre-aggregation placement matters.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b) replaces the raw-sequence variation with $D'_j=|A_j-A_{j-1}|$ while retaining the residual, graph mixture, and remaining settings. The pre-aggregation classifier, unchanged by Stage~2, achieves 86.66\% AUC compared with 85.86\% for post-aggregation variation, a $0.81\pm0.18$ point advantage. Because the added module is otherwise the same, this comparison identifies pre-aggregation placement as a material design choice. The valid inputs coincide on short, padded training sequences; the variants differ on averaged training sequences and at test-window boundaries. The result is consistent with the intended role of supplying explicit raw-sequence variation to the temporal model.

\textbf{The semantic objective improves adaptation.}
The equal-budget control C in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) updates the same $\mlp_1$ parameters for three epochs from the same Stage~1 checkpoint, but uses CLASM alone ($\lambda=0$). Adding the binary semantic objective increases AP from 77.59\% to 79.23\%, a paired gain of $1.64\pm0.43$ points. This isolates the contribution of anomaly supervision from the additional optimization budget. Applying Stage~2 directly to VadCLIP reaches 79.15\% AP, compared with 79.23\% for the complete two-stage model. The two refinements contribute to different prediction objectives: pre-aggregation temporal refinement improves classification-based anomaly detection on UCF-Crime, while pathway-restricted adaptation drives the semantic AP improvement on XD-Violence.

\textbf{The classifier is preserved during Stage~2.}
The classification AP in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c) remains 79.54\% for the Stage~1, control C, and full Stage~2 checkpoints. Fixed-input checks during Stage~2 additionally record zero change in classification logits and no changed parameter outside $\mlp_1$. These observations agree with Eq.~\eqref{eq:invariance}: the added objective adapts the semantic pathway without modifying the classifier that supplies the conditioning weights.

\subsection{Efficiency and Practical Integration}
Table~\ref{tab:complexity} reports measurements on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Laptop GPU. Each timing is the median of 200 forward passes after 20 warm-up passes, with CUDA synchronization. The measured network takes precomputed appearance and variation tensors and includes prompt encoding; visual feature extraction and variation preprocessing are outside the timed region.

Stage~1 adds 132,736 parameters. Neural scoring latency increases from 28.88 to 29.84~ms per UCF-Crime window and from 17.44 to 18.06~ms per XD-Violence window. Using the unrounded measurements, these increases are 3.3\% and 3.6\%. Peak allocated memory increases by approximately 1.01~MiB. Stage~2 only changes the values of an existing adapter, so it adds no inference parameters or operations to the Stage~1 model.

The framework separates temporal representation refinement from semantic adaptation in a way that supports integration into a video-analysis pipeline. PVR supplies aligned variation features without replacing the CLIP encoders or prediction heads. Semantic adaptation can then improve prompt-aligned scores while preserving classification-based alerts associated with the Stage~1 checkpoint. This property is useful when anomaly scores feed an established monitoring or human-review workflow: semantic refinement does not itself require recalibrating thresholds on the unchanged classification output. The experiments demonstrate this output-preserving property and its low scoring overhead within VadCLIP.
