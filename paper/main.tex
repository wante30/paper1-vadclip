\title{Pre-Aggregation Temporal Variation and Pathway-Restricted Semantic Adaptation for Weakly Supervised Video Anomaly Detection}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
CLIP-based weakly supervised video anomaly detectors resample each training video to a fixed length before any temporal modelling, and segment-mean aggregation suppresses the local temporal variation inside long videos. Optimizing an additional semantic objective over the whole network also perturbs the classification pathway on which other predictions depend. We address both problems by deciding where refinement is applied. Pre-aggregation temporal refinement computes snippet differences on the raw sequence, aggregates them with the same segment boundaries as the appearance features, and injects them through a zero-initialized low-rank residual. Pathway-restricted semantic adaptation optimizes a semantic multiple-instance objective only on the semantic-side adapter, after the classification and semantic pathways separate, so the classification output is preserved. Built on VadCLIP and compared with VadCLIP retrained under the same fixed-epoch protocol and matched seeds, the method improves UCF-Crime AUC by \ucfGainMean{} points and XD-Violence AP by \xdGainMean{} points, with positive paired differences on all three seeds. Under VadCLIP's official training setting, the XD-Violence gain is \offGainMean{}$\pm$\offGainSd{} points, positive on \offGainPos{} runs. The method adds \pvrParams{} parameters and increases inference latency by \cxUcfLatRel--\cxXdLatRel\%.
\end{abstract}
